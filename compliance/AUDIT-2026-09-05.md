## Headline

The site is in **better shape than a typical RUO peptide storefront**. The foundational
controls the reviewer looks for — age gate, sitewide RUO disclaimers, no calculators, no
dosing instructions, no self-administration guidance — are already in place and passed.

The exposure is concentrated in **one theme**: pages that present **human clinical
weight-loss outcome figures as headline marketing statistics**. That is the single most
likely reason this site fails the scan, and it is a contained fix — 13 pages, no product
data, no checkout, no structural change.

One separate item is a **P0** because it is damaging in front of a reviewer for reasons
that have nothing to do with peptides: five live pages publish an internal build note
saying the content was "sanitized."

### What already passes — do not "fix" these

Verified present and working; listed so nobody spends remediation budget here:

- **Age gate** — real modal, 21+ confirmation, cookie (`opb_age_verified`, 1-year) plus
  `localStorage` persistence, with an explicit decline path. Satisfies §2 *Age gate*.
- **Sitewide RUO disclaimers** — footer and header carry "FOR LABORATORY RESEARCH USE ONLY",
  "NOT FOR HUMAN OR ANIMAL CONSUMPTION", "Not a drug, dietary supplement, or cosmetic",
  and "PRODUCTS ARE NOT INTENDED TO DIAGNOSE, TREAT, CURE, OR PREVENT ANY DISEASE."
- **No calculators or interactive dosing tools** — zero matches sitewide for peptide/dose/
  reconstitution/syringe calculators or unit converters. Clean pass on §1 and §2.
- **No dosing, administration, or self-administration instructions** — no mg/kg or IU
  protocols, injection routes, frequency schedules, or "how to use" content. The
  `dose`/`administration` hits in the raw scan are laboratory storage and assay language
  (aliquots, −20 °C, washout intervals in crossover design), which is legitimate RUO content.
- **No cycle, stack, or personal-use protocol content** — clean. The one dosing-adjacent
  match sitewide is "Route studied: Subcutaneous models" on `/half-life-pharmacokinetics-hub/`,
  which describes a preclinical model, not human administration. Defensible as written.
- **Product-page FAQ pattern** — nearly every product page asks "Is *X* intended for human
  use?" and answers no. This is a strength; keep it.
- **Research Partner Program pages** (this repo's own three pages) — clean. Every match is
  *prohibitive* language ("No dosing…", "Do not publish…").

## P1 — Human clinical weight-loss figures used as marketing statistics

This is the finding that matters. The requirement doc, §1:

> **No Human Benefit / Treatment Claims** — Do not make personal-benefit, **weight-loss**,
> muscle-building, anti-aging, therapeutic, disease-treatment, or similar outcome claims.
> Research positioning must remain consistent throughout the website.

The content is factually accurate and correctly cited to published literature. That does not
save it. A compliance scanner — and a human underwriter — reads a hero stat block rendering

> **~28.7%** Retatrutide Weight Loss (68wk)  **~22%** Tirzepatide Weight Loss (72wk)

on a page that links straight to an add-to-cart button as a weight-loss claim attached to a
purchasable product. The citation is what makes it *worse*, not better: it supplies the
efficacy number the customer is actually shopping for.

### The three shapes to remove

**1. Hero stat blocks.** Large-type numbers whose label is a human outcome.

| Remove | Replace with |
|--------|--------------|
| `~28.7%` **Retatrutide Weight Loss (68wk)** | `3` **Receptor targets** (GLP-1R / GIPR / GCGR) |
| `~22%` **Tirzepatide Weight Loss (72wk)** | `2` **Receptor targets** (GLP-1R / GIPR) |
| `~15%` **Semaglutide Weight Loss** | `1` **Receptor target** (GLP-1R) |
| `−16.34 kg` **Retatrutide (meta)** | *(delete tile)* |
| `−11.82 kg` **Tirzepatide (meta)** | *(delete tile)* |
| `~7%` **Extra weight loss vs Sema** | `+GIPR` **Added receptor arm** |

Receptor count is the honest differentiator between these compounds and is the thing a
laboratory buyer actually selects on. It carries the comparison without an outcome claim.

**2. Comparison-table columns.** Delete the entire column, header and cells:

- `Peak Weight Loss` — `/metabolic-peptide-comparison/`, `/triple-agonist-peptides/`,
  `/what-is-retatrutide/`, `/tirzepatide-vs-semaglutide-research/`
- `Weight loss (highest dose, ~72wk)` and `Absolute weight reduction (meta-analysis)` —
  `/retatrutide-vs-tirzepatide/`
- `Body Weight Reduction`, `Responders >15% loss` — `/semaglutide-vs-tirzepatide/`
- `Published Efficacy Data Comparison` table — `/retatrutide-vs-tirzepatide-research/`

Keep the receptor/mechanism/half-life/format rows. Those are the legitimate RUO comparison
and they are the majority of every one of these tables.

**3. Body-copy efficacy sentences.** Rewrite to describe *receptor pharmacology*, not
*human outcomes*. Worked examples — these are drop-in replacements:

> **Before** — `/cagrilintide-vs-semaglutide/`
> CagriSema is Novo Nordisk's fixed-ratio combination of cagrilintide and semaglutide in
> Phase 3 trials (REDEFINE program). Phase 2 data published in NEJM (2023) showed ~15.6% body
> weight reduction with CagriSema vs ~8% for cagrilintide alone and ~5.1% for semaglutide at
> matched doses — suggesting genuine additive or synergistic interaction.
>
> **After**
> CagriSema is a fixed-ratio combination of a long-acting amylin analog and a GLP-1 receptor
> agonist. It is the reference model for studying amylin-receptor and GLP-1R signalling in
> parallel: the two components act on non-overlapping receptor families, which is why
> co-administration models are used to investigate additive versus independent pathway
> activity in vitro.

> **Before** — `/cjc-1295-vs-tesamorelin/`
> The PRIME trial (tesamorelin for visceral adiposity) provides a key published dataset:
> 26-week treatment produced ~15% visceral adipose tissue reduction and significant IGF-1
> elevation above baseline.
>
> **After**
> Tesamorelin is a well-characterised GHRH analog with an extensively documented
> pharmacology profile, which makes it a useful reference compound for GH/IGF-1 axis
> assay work. Published datasets report measurable IGF-1 elevation, giving researchers a
> known-response benchmark when validating GH-axis readouts.

> **Before** — `/retatrutide-vs-tirzepatide/`
> This receptor balance is hypothesized to be the source of retatrutide's superior weight
> loss efficacy in published Phase 2 data.
>
> **After**
> This receptor balance — the addition of GCGR agonism to the GLP-1R/GIPR pair — is the
> mechanistic difference researchers isolate when designing triple- versus dual-agonist
> comparison studies.

> **Before** — `/research-peptides-houston/`
> …its published Phase 2 clinical data showing ~28.7% mean body weight reduction at 68 weeks
> — the highest recorded for any single compound in this class.
>
> **After**
> …its triple-receptor profile (GLP-1R / GIPR / GCGR), the broadest receptor coverage of any
> compound in this class and the reason it is used as a reference agonist in multi-pathway
> study designs.

**Rule of thumb for the remaining instances:** if a sentence contains a percentage or a
kilogram figure attached to a body-composition noun (*weight, body weight, adipose, fat*),
it comes out. Describe what the molecule *binds*, not what happened to *people*.

## P2 — AI-generated product imagery

**20 distinct image files, served across all 40 product pages**, with filenames like:

```
/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ChatGPT-Image-Aug-26-2026-02_17_23-PM.png
/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ChatGPT-Image-Aug-25-2026-03_59_12-PM.png
```

These appear in `src`, `srcset`, `data-thumb` and gallery attributes, so they are visible in
page source and to any crawler. §2 of the requirement doc puts *"images, vial labels,
graphics… and product presentation"* squarely in scope, and imagery that is provably not a
photograph of the product being sold is a presentation problem independent of the peptide
rules.

**Fix:** re-upload under neutral filenames derived from the SKU
(`op-met-reta-10mg-vial.png`), and replace with photographs of actual inventory where
possible. Renaming alone removes the signal; real photos remove the underlying issue. Update
the WooCommerce media references — do not just rename files on disk or every product image
404s.

## Caveats

- This audit reflects **anonymous-visitor HTML on 2026-09-05**. Content behind the age gate
  or a logged-in research account was not evaluated, and neither were PDFs in
  `/documentation-downloads/`, the Elementor popup library, or email templates — all of which
  §2 and §5 put in scope.
- The scanner finds *language patterns*. It does not certify compliance, and it cannot
  predict what the VERIFIED plugin flags. §5 makes ongoing monitoring the merchant's
  obligation; treat `scan.py` as a pre-submission check, not a substitute for the review.
- Nothing on the live site has been changed by this audit.
