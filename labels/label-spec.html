Product identity is carried by chemical designation and CAS number only — nothing on these labels states a dose, a route, or an effect.

§1

How mock_E fits each die

One thing to settle before anything is cut. The mock_E layout needs about 62 mm of width to hold its three batch fields at legible type — OP-MET-RETA-10MG alone is 16 characters, and at the 5 pt floor that is 17 mm before the other two fields and their gutters. A 2R vial is only 50 mm around. So the template runs at full strength on the large vial and the carton, and takes two documented cuts on the small vial.

The one decision that changes the dies

  • Which vial are the four 5–10 mg products actually in? This pack assumes a 2R (Ø16 × 35 mm), which forces Die A's compressions. If they are in a 10R (Ø24 × 45 mm) like the Glutathione, Die A disappears — all five vials run on Die B at full mock_E, one die instead of two, and one less tooling charge. Measure one vial and tell us the outside diameter.
  • A 1 mm error in vial diameter is a 3.1 mm error in label width. Measure, do not assume.

Two-letter product code, year and month of manufacture, then a three-digit sequence within that month. Nothing on the printed label carries this — it is overprinted at fill.

Cap-seal colours are a suggestion, not a requirement. Flip-off seals cost the same in any stock colour, and they make a mis-picked vial obvious on the bench before the label is ever read.

Confirm before tooling

  • Die A's vertical safe zone is 1.5 mm, not 2 mm. On a 20 mm label the mock_E stack will not fit inside 2 mm, and vertical registration on a wrap applicator is the easy axis. Tell us if your press cannot hold it.
  • Die C is sized for an 84 × 40 mm carton face. Send the carton's real face dimensions before the die is cut.
  • Three dies means three one-time tooling charges — quote die and plate charges separately from the per-thousand price. If §1's vial question collapses Die A into Die B, that drops to two.

Print specification

Face stockMatte white BOPP, 50 µm (2 mil). Not paper — these vials get condensation coming out of the freezer.
AdhesivePermanent acrylic, cryogenic grade. Service range −80 °C to +40 °C, minimum application temperature +5 °C. Hot-melt rubber adhesive is not acceptable — these ship on cold packs and store at −20 °C, and rubber releases in the freezer.
Liner1 mil PET or glassine, roll form.
Print methodNarrow-web flexo or digital (HP Indigo class). Digital preferred at these run lengths — no plate charge across five SKUs.
Colours — two inks Charcoal flood #1B1C22 · one spot violet.
All white text and rules are knockouts to the bare white substrate — do not print white ink, and do not build the charcoal as a rich black behind fine reversed type.
Spot violet Target #9B4DF5, with the deeper rule tint at #7B2FD4 — the same spot at 80 % plus 15 % K, not a second plate.
This colour is outside CMYK gamut and prints grey and dull if built from process inks. It must run as a spot. Nearest common guide reference to start from is Pantone 2592 C — match against a physical guide and send a drawdown before plate release. Tolerance ΔE ≤ 2.0.
FinishMatte UV varnish or matte lamination over the full face. Must survive 30 wipes with 70 % IPA without ink lift or gloss change — vials are swabbed before entering a hood.
Minimum type5.0 pt (1.76 mm). Minimum rule weight 0.25 pt. Nothing in this pack is set smaller.
Artwork supplied asVector PDF/X-4, fonts outlined, spot named OP Violet, die-line on a separate CutContour spot layer set to overprint.
Variable dataLeft blank. Lot, SKU and manufacture date are assigned at fill, after the labels are printed, so the dotted rules stay empty and are overprinted in-house — thermal transfer, resin ribbon (wax will not survive the IPA wipe). Do not print them on press.
Barcode zoneDie C only — 41 × 11 mm clear area, GS1-128 carrying (01) GTIN, (10) lot, (17) expiry. X-dimension 0.25 mm, quiet zone 10 × X, grade ≥ 1.5 / C per ISO 15416. Leave unprinted until the GS1 prefix is issued.
Roll formatWound out, position 1 (label reads left-to-right, top edge leaves the roll first). 76 mm (3 in) core, 300 mm maximum OD, 1 000 labels per roll, 3 mm gap. Required for automated wrap application.
TolerancesDie-cut registration ±0.5 mm · print-to-die registration ±0.3 mm · splices ≤ 1 per roll, flagged.
Pre-productionRequired before the full run: printed proof on the actual substrate, plus a freeze/thaw adhesion test — 5 cycles between −20 °C and +25 °C on a glass vial, no edge lift.

§8

Verify before plate release

Placeholders and inferences in the artwork above

  • Vial outside diameter for the four small SKUs — the §1 question. Everything else waits on it.
  • The typeface. These proofs are set in Archivo as a stand-in. Production PDFs must be generated from your mock_E source file so the real face is preserved, then outlined.
  • The violet and the charcoal were sampled by eye from the mock_E PNG. Pull the exact values from the source file before a spot colour is mixed.
  • Every CAS number. The values shown are the commonly cited ones, but salt and acetate forms carry different registry numbers. Check each against the supplier's Certificate of Analysis — a wrong CAS on a printed label is a full reprint.
  • The purity figure. Artwork reads ≥ 99.0 % (HPLC) throughout. If any CoA states a lower specification, that SKU's label changes to match it.
  • The SKU category codes (MET / COS / REP / ANT) are inferred from the single OP-MET-TIRZ-20MG example. Send the real code list.
  • The shortened vial SKU. A 42 mm label cannot hold the full code at 5 pt, so Die A carries product and strength only (RETA-10MG) while the carton carries the full OP-MET-RETA-10MG. Confirm that split works for your stock system.
  • The research-use wording. Vials carry your mock's "NOT FOR HUMAN USE"; the carton has room for the longer form used on your compliance page, which also excludes veterinary use. Confirm you want both.
  • GTIN / GS1 company prefix, or confirmation that the Die C barcode zone stays blank this run.
